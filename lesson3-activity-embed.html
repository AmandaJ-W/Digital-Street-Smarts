Lesson 3 Activity

What went wrong?

Read John's story and spot the mistakes.

1 of 3

John's story: Part 1

Last Tuesday, John received a phone call from someone who said they were from his bank's fraud prevention department. The caller said there was suspicious activity on his account and asked him to confirm his account number and PIN to verify his identity. John was alarmed, so he provided the information. After he hung up, he decided to wait a few days to see if any unauthorized charges showed up.

What should John have done right after hanging up?

2 of 3

John's story: Part 2

A few days later, John noticed a charge he did not recognize. He remembered that the caller had left him a callback number, so he called it to report the problem.

Was calling that number a good idea?

3 of 3

John's story: Part 3

John felt embarrassed about what had happened. He did not tell his family, did not report it to anyone, and did not change any of his passwords. He reasoned that the scammer only had his bank information, not his email or other accounts.

John made two mistakes here. Which of the following is one of them?

Your results

What John should have done

  • Called his bank immediately using the number on the back of his card. He should not have waited, and should not have called back a number given by the caller.
  • Changed his passwords right away. Scammers often use one piece of information to access other accounts.
  • Reported the scam to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov and the FBI at ic3.gov.
  • Told someone he trusted. Being scammed can happen to anyone. There is nothing to be ashamed of.
